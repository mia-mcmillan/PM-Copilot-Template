# Strategic Questioning Pattern

**Purpose:** Library of context-appropriate questions that guide deeper thinking, uncover insights, and strengthen product decisions.

## Why Questions Matter

Great product managers ask great questions. Strategic questions:
- **Uncover hidden assumptions:** Surface beliefs treated as facts
- **Expand thinking:** Explore alternatives not initially considered
- **Drive clarity:** Force articulation of fuzzy ideas
- **Build alignment:** Ensure team shares understanding
- **Prevent groupthink:** Challenge consensus when needed
- **Enable discovery:** Help users find their own insights (stronger than being told)

**Good strategic questioning** helps users think better, not just faster.

## Principles of Strategic Questioning

### 1. Questions Over Answers
When possible, ask rather than tell. Users internalize insights they discover themselves.

L **Telling:** "You're assuming customers want this feature"
 **Asking:** "What evidence validates customer demand for this?"

### 2. Context-Appropriate Timing
Right question at wrong time is wrong question. Match question to user's current thinking stage.

- **Too early:** Asking "what's the MVP?" before problem is validated
- **Too late:** Asking "what problem are we solving?" when engineering is building
- **Just right:** "What would validate this hypothesis?" when exploring opportunity

### 3. Progressive Depth
Start broad, narrow based on answers. Don't jump to tactical questions before strategic clarity.

```
Level 1: "What problem are we solving?"
  “ (based on answer)
Level 2: "Who specifically has this problem?"
  “ (based on answer)
Level 3: "How are they solving it today?"
  “ (based on answer)
Level 4: "What would make our solution 10x better than current approach?"
```

### 4. Balance Challenge with Support
Questions should stretch thinking, not break confidence. Mix validating and challenging questions.

**Challenging only:**
- "What evidence supports that?"
- "How do you know customers want this?"
- "Why would this work when competitors failed?"
[Feels adversarial]

**Balanced:**
- "This direction is interesting - what evidence supports it?" [validating + challenging]
- "I see the customer need - what would differentiate us?" [validating + expanding]

### 5. Genuinely Curious
Ask questions you want answered, not rhetorical gotchas. Genuine curiosity invites collaboration.

L **Rhetorical:** "Don't you think we should validate this first?" [Answer obvious]
 **Curious:** "What validation would give you confidence to proceed?" [Open exploration]

## Question Library by Context

### Opportunity Assessment

**Problem Definition:**
- What problem are we solving?
- Who specifically has this problem? (segment, persona, context)
- How painful is this problem? (nice-to-have vs. critical)
- How are customers solving this today?
- What's the cost of the problem? (time, money, frustration)
- Why is this problem worth solving now?

**Customer Understanding (JTBD):**
- What job is the customer trying to get done?
- What are they hiring to do this job today?
- What makes current solutions inadequate?
- What would cause them to fire current solution and hire ours?
- What progress are they trying to make?
- What social, emotional, and functional dimensions matter?

**Solution Validation:**
- What evidence validates customers want our solution?
- Have we talked to customers who have this problem?
- What did they say in their own words? (not our interpretation)
- Is this stated preference or observed behavior?
- Would customers pay for this solution? How much?
- What would cause them NOT to adopt our solution?

**Alternative Exploration:**
- What other ways could we solve this problem?
- What would happen if we did nothing?
- What's the simplest possible solution?
- What would a 10x better solution look like?
- How would [competitor/adjacent market] approach this?

### Strategy Formation

**Vision & Positioning:**
- What's our long-term product vision? (3-5 year future state)
- What makes this product defensible?
- Why should customers choose us over alternatives?
- What category are we creating or leading?
- How do we want to be positioned in customers' minds?
- What do we want to be known for?

**Strategic Themes:**
- What are our 3-5 key strategic themes for this period?
- What customer outcomes do these themes enable?
- How do these themes differentiate us?
- What are we saying no to? (equally important)
- How do themes connect to company goals?
- What success metrics validate we're executing the strategy?

**Market Dynamics:**
- What trends are shaping the market?
- How is customer behavior changing?
- What are competitors doing that's working/not working?
- Where are competitors weak that we could exploit?
- What makes this market attractive?
- What would disrupt our market position?

**Strategic Trade-offs:**
- What are we optimizing for? (speed, quality, scope, cost)
- What must be true for this strategy to succeed?
- What's the biggest risk to this strategy?
- What would cause us to change direction?
- What can only we do? (unique advantages)
- What constraints are we accepting? (intentional limitations)

### Prioritization

**Impact Assessment:**
- What customer outcome does this enable?
- How many customers does this affect?
- Which customer segments specifically?
- What's the expected business impact? (revenue, retention, efficiency)
- How do we measure success?
- What happens if we don't build this?

**Opportunity Cost:**
- What are we NOT doing if we do this?
- What's the cost of delaying other opportunities?
- Is this the highest-value use of engineering time?
- What could we build instead with the same resources?
- What's irreversible about this decision?

**Strategic Alignment:**
- How does this align with strategic themes?
- Does this move us toward our vision?
- Is this a strategic bet or tactical fix?
- Would this still be important if we achieved our vision?
- Does this differentiate us or just keep pace?

**Feasibility & Risk:**
- How confident are we in the estimate?
- What could go wrong?
- What dependencies exist?
- What do we need to learn first?
- What's the MVP we could ship to validate?
- Can we de-risk this incrementally?

**Sequencing:**
- Why now? Why not earlier or later?
- What must be built first?
- Does this enable future opportunities?
- Is this a platform play or point solution?
- How does this fit in the roadmap narrative?

### Solution Design

**Scope Definition:**
- What's in scope for V1? V2? Never?
- What's the minimum viable solution?
- What can we learn without building?
- What makes this "done"?
- What are we explicitly not solving?
- Why is this the right scope?

**User Experience:**
- How does this fit in the user's workflow?
- What existing user behaviors can we leverage?
- What new behaviors are we requiring?
- What makes this delightful vs. just functional?
- Where will users get stuck?
- How do we onboard users to this?

**Architecture & Technical:**
- What technical approaches did we consider?
- Why this architecture over alternatives?
- What technical debt are we creating?
- What constraints are we working within?
- How does this scale?
- What's the maintenance burden?

**Success Metrics:**
- How will we know this succeeded?
- What metrics will move if this works?
- What's the current baseline?
- What's the target and timeframe?
- What leading indicators can we track?
- What would cause us to kill this if metrics disappoint?

### Execution Planning

**Requirements Clarity:**
- What's the user story / job to be done?
- What are we assuming about user needs?
- What edge cases exist?
- What's the happy path? What are alternatives?
- What questions do we need to answer before building?
- Where is there ambiguity?

**Delivery Approach:**
- What's the phased rollout plan?
- Can we ship to subset of users first?
- How do we de-risk the launch?
- What can break? What's the blast radius?
- What's the rollback plan?
- How do we gather feedback?

**Team Alignment:**
- Does engineering understand the "why"?
- Does design have the context needed?
- What questions does the team have?
- Where do we need more clarity?
- What decisions are still open?
- Who needs to review this?

**Launch Readiness:**
- What must be true to launch?
- What risks remain unaddressed?
- Is documentation ready?
- Is support team trained?
- How will we announce this?
- What customer communication is needed?

### Retrospective & Learning

**Outcome Assessment:**
- Did we achieve what we set out to do?
- What metrics moved? By how much?
- What surprised us?
- What did we learn about customers?
- What did we learn about our product?

**Decision Quality:**
- Were our assumptions validated?
- What would we do differently?
- Where was our reasoning sound? Where was it flawed?
- What did we over-invest in? Under-invest in?
- What signals did we miss?

**Process Improvement:**
- What slowed us down?
- What went smoothly?
- How could we have de-risked this better?
- What would help us make better decisions next time?
- What should we start/stop/continue doing?

### Stakeholder Management

**Understanding Perspectives:**
- What does [stakeholder] care about?
- What are their goals and constraints?
- What concerns do they have?
- What would success look like to them?
- What information do they need to decide?

**Building Alignment:**
- Where do we agree? Where do we disagree?
- What data would resolve disagreements?
- What trade-offs are we making?
- How can we find a solution that addresses multiple needs?
- Who else needs to weigh in?

**Managing Conflict:**
- What's the root cause of the disagreement?
- What are we optimizing for differently?
- Can we reframe the problem?
- What experiments would give us data to decide?
- How do we escalate if we can't align?

## Mode-Specific Questioning Styles

### JDI Mode
**Style:** Minimal, clarifying questions only

Ask when:
- Ambiguity would derail execution
- Quick answer unlocks progress
- Critical context is missing

Don't ask when:
- User has clear direction
- Question would slow momentum
- Answer won't change approach

Example questions:
- "Just to confirm - you want X, not Y?"
- "Which customer segment is this for?"
- "What's the success metric?"
- "Any constraints I should know about?"

### Consult Mode
**Style:** Proactive, expansive strategic questions

Ask liberally to:
- Deepen strategic thinking
- Surface alternatives
- Challenge assumptions
- Strengthen arguments

Example questions:
- "What customer evidence supports this direction?"
- "What alternatives did you consider?"
- "What makes this defensible long-term?"
- "What would cause you to change this decision?"
- "How does this connect to strategic themes?"

### Socratic Mode
**Style:** Questions only, no statements

**Never:**
- State observations ("I notice you're assuming...")
- Provide answers ("Here's what the data shows...")
- Give advice ("You should validate this...")

**Always:**
- Ask questions that lead to insight
- Follow answers with deeper questions
- Use questions to surface contradictions
- Build question sequences that guide discovery

Example sequence:
```
User: "We should build a mobile app"
Socratic:
1. "What problem would the mobile app solve?"
2. "Who has this problem?"
3. "How are they solving it today?"
4. "What evidence shows they need a mobile solution?"
5. "What would make them adopt our app over current solution?"
6. "What makes now the right time for this investment?"
7. "What else could we build with those same resources?"
8. "How would you prioritize mobile app vs. those alternatives?"
```

### Challenge Mode
**Style:** Aggressive questioning, stress-testing

Ask to:
- Expose weaknesses in reasoning
- Surface unstated assumptions
- Identify blind spots
- Prepare for stakeholder challenges

Example questions:
- "What if you're wrong about this?"
- "Why would customers choose us when [competitor] has this?"
- "What evidence disproves your hypothesis?"
- "What's the worst-case scenario?"
- "How would [skeptical stakeholder] attack this proposal?"
- "What are you not seeing that could derail this?"

### Facilitate Mode
**Style:** Questions that drive group alignment

Ask to:
- Surface diverse perspectives
- Build shared understanding
- Identify consensus and gaps
- Guide collaborative decisions

Example questions:
- "What does each stakeholder need from this decision?"
- "Where do we have alignment? Where do we differ?"
- "What information would help everyone feel confident?"
- "What concerns haven't been voiced yet?"
- "How can we test this hypothesis together?"

### Teach Mode
**Style:** Questions that build PM skills

Ask to:
- Demonstrate thinking frameworks
- Build pattern recognition
- Strengthen judgment
- Transfer mental models

Example questions:
- "What framework could help you think through this?"
- "How would a seasoned PM approach this?"
- "What patterns do you notice across these opportunities?"
- "What questions should you be asking that you aren't?"
- "How will you know you made the right decision?"

## Question Sequences (Progressive Depth)

### Sequence 1: Opportunity Validation
```
Layer 1 - Problem Clarity:
"What problem are we solving?"
  “
Layer 2 - Customer Specificity:
"Who specifically has this problem?"
  “
Layer 3 - Current State:
"How are they solving it today? Why is that inadequate?"
  “
Layer 4 - Solution Validation:
"What evidence shows they would adopt our solution?"
  “
Layer 5 - Business Case:
"What's the business impact if we solve this?"
```

### Sequence 2: Strategic Direction
```
Layer 1 - Vision:
"What's our long-term vision for this product?"
  “
Layer 2 - Positioning:
"How do we want to be positioned vs. competitors?"
  “
Layer 3 - Differentiation:
"What makes this defensible?"
  “
Layer 4 - Focus:
"What are we saying no to in order to achieve this?"
  “
Layer 5 - Success:
"What metrics validate we're on the right path?"
```

### Sequence 3: Build vs. Not Build
```
Layer 1 - Impact:
"What customer outcome does this enable?"
  “
Layer 2 - Evidence:
"What evidence validates customers need this?"
  “
Layer 3 - Alternatives:
"What else could we build instead?"
  “
Layer 4 - Opportunity Cost:
"What's the cost of NOT building those alternatives?"
  “
Layer 5 - Decision:
"Given the trade-offs, why is this the right choice?"
```

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

### L Anti-Pattern 1: Interrogation
**Problem:** Feels like cross-examination, not collaboration

Bad example:
```
- "What's the evidence?"
- "Why do you think that?"
- "How do you know?"
- "What makes you confident?"
- "Where's the data?"
[Relentless, no breathing room]
```

**Better approach:** Mix questions with validation
```
"This direction is interesting. What evidence supports it?"
[User answers]
"That makes sense. What alternatives did you consider?"
```

### L Anti-Pattern 2: Rhetorical Questions
**Problem:** Not real questions, just disguised statements

Bad example:
```
- "Don't you think we should validate first?" [Really saying: validate first]
- "Wouldn't customers want X instead?" [Really saying: build X]
- "Shouldn't we talk to users?" [Really saying: talk to users]
```

**Better approach:** Ask genuine questions
```
"What validation would give you confidence to proceed?"
"What do customers want - X or Y?"
"What would we learn from talking to users?"
```

### L Anti-Pattern 3: Question Dumping
**Problem:** Overwhelming barrage of questions

Bad example:
```
"What problem are we solving? Who has the problem? How many customers?
What's the revenue impact? How long will it take? What are the risks?
What alternatives exist? Why this over those? How will we measure success?
What could go wrong?"
[10 questions without waiting for answers]
```

**Better approach:** Progressive questioning
```
"What problem are we solving?"
[User answers]
"Got it. Who specifically has this problem?"
[User answers]
"Interesting. How are they solving it today?"
[Build understanding through sequence]
```

### L Anti-Pattern 4: Asking Questions You Can Answer
**Problem:** Wastes time, user expects you to contribute knowledge

Bad example:
```
User: "What was our Q3 churn rate?"
Claude: "What do you think the churn rate was?"
[I can query Snowflake, should just answer]
```

**Better approach:** Provide answers when you can, question when you can't
```
User: "What was our Q3 churn rate?"
Claude: [Queries Snowflake] "Q3 churn was 8%. Is that higher than expected?"
```

### L Anti-Pattern 5: Leading Questions
**Problem:** Manipulates user toward predetermined answer

Bad example:
```
"Given that customers clearly want feature X [assumption embedded],
what's preventing us from building it?"
[Question presumes conclusion]
```

**Better approach:** Open questions
```
"What evidence shows customer demand for feature X?"
[Let evidence determine conclusion]
```

### L Anti-Pattern 6: Premature Detail Questions
**Problem:** Tactical questions before strategic clarity

Bad example:
```
User: "I'm thinking about mobile app strategy"
Claude: "What color should the primary button be?"
[Way too detailed for strategic discussion]
```

**Better approach:** Match question depth to thinking stage
```
User: "I'm thinking about mobile app strategy"
Claude: "What's the strategic thesis for mobile?
What job would a mobile app do that web doesn't?"
```

## When to Ask vs. When to Tell

### Ask Questions When:
- User has context you lack
- Multiple valid approaches exist
- Discovery will strengthen buy-in
- User needs to develop judgment
- Strategic thinking needed
- Answer will guide next steps

### Provide Answers When:
- You have data user lacks (Snowflake, Gong)
- Clear best practice exists
- Time pressure requires efficiency
- User explicitly requests guidance
- Technical knowledge needed
- Question would waste time

### Hybrid Approach (Best)
Provide data + ask strategic question:
```
"I searched Gong and found 15 mentions of feature X in 200 calls.
Given that 7.5% mention rate, how would you prioritize this vs.
other opportunities?"
[Data + strategic question]
```

## Measuring Question Effectiveness

Good questions lead to:
- **Deeper thinking:** User pauses, reflects
- **New insights:** "Oh, I hadn't thought about that"
- **Better decisions:** Changes approach based on clarity
- **Self-discovery:** User answers their own question
- **Engagement:** User energized by exploration

Bad questions lead to:
- **Defensiveness:** User feels attacked
- **Confusion:** User doesn't understand question
- **Frustration:** "Just tell me what to do"
- **Shallow responses:** Quick, un-reflected answers
- **Disengagement:** User stops collaborating

Adjust questioning style based on these signals.

## Quick Reference

| Context | Key Questions | Pattern Intensity by Mode |
|---------|--------------|--------------------------|
| **Opportunity** | What problem? Who has it? How validated? | JDI: Low / Consult: High |
| **Strategy** | What's defensible? What do we say no to? | JDI: Minimal / Consult: High |
| **Prioritization** | What's the opportunity cost? Why now? | JDI: Low / Consult: High |
| **Execution** | What's the MVP? How do we de-risk? | JDI: Medium / Consult: Medium |
| **Retrospective** | What did we learn? What would we change? | All modes: Medium |

**Golden rule:** Ask questions that help users think better, not questions that prove you're smart.
