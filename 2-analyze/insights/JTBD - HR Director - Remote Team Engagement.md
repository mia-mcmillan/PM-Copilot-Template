# HR Director - Jobs-to-be-Done Analysis: Remote Team Engagement

**Date**: 2025-11-16
**Customer Segment**: HR Directors at Remote-First Companies (400-800 employees)
**Persona**: Sarah Martinez (Acme Corp - External, fictional example)
**Researcher**: Product Team
**Sources**: [[2025-11-15 - Acme Corp - HR Director - JTBD]]

## Executive Summary

Sarah Martinez is the Director of People Operations at Acme Corp, a 500-employee remote-first B2B SaaS company. Her primary challenge is maintaining company culture and reducing turnover in a distributed workforce where employees feel increasingly disconnected. Traditional wellness solutions (gym reimbursements, virtual happy hours, generic wellness platforms) have failed to drive engagement, with participation dropping from 80% to 20% over time.

**Key Pain Points** (ranked by severity):
1. **Critical**: Unable to demonstrate wellness program ROI, facing $120K budget cut in Q1 2026
2. **High**: Remote employees cite "feeling isolated" as #2 reason for leaving (contributing to 18% annual turnover)
3. **High**: One-size-fits-all wellness programs fail diverse employee needs (fitness levels, time, equipment)
4. **Medium**: Lack of organic team bonding opportunities across 6 time zones
5. **Medium**: Admin burden of managing multiple wellness vendors and generic communications

**Strategic Opportunity**: Sarah needs a solution that creates team bonding through wellness without requiring synchronous Zoom calls, works across all fitness levels, and provides measurable ROI data she can present to skeptical executives. She has a compelling event (budget renewal) and decision authority up to $75K/year.

**Competitive Context**: Previously used Gympass (too expensive, only 22% adoption), currently using Wellable (31% MAU, declining engagement, contract expires Dec 31), evaluated Peloton (equipment cost prohibitive). Strongly prefers solutions that don't require employee equipment purchases and can demonstrate clear participation metrics.

## Job Statement

**When** remote employees feel disconnected and lonely, impacting company culture and retention,
**I want to** create engaging team bonding opportunities through wellness activities that work asynchronously across time zones,
**So I can** maintain company culture, reduce turnover, and demonstrate measurable ROI to justify wellness program spend.

### Example Context

> "We're a remote-first company, but that shouldn't mean people feel alone. I need something that brings teams together around wellness without another damn Zoom meeting."

> "I have 4 weeks to either prove Wellable is working better than I can currently show, or find something new. Otherwise, we're canceling the program entirely, and I think that would be terrible for morale."

## Job Dimensions

### Functional Job

**The Practical Task**: Create regular, engaging team activities that foster connections between remote employees while accommodating different time zones, fitness levels, and personal constraints.

**Current Solution**:
1. **Virtual happy hours** (abandoned) - 80% → 20% attendance over 8 weeks, Zoom fatigue
2. **Gym membership reimbursement** ($50/month) - 28% utilization, doesn't build team bonds
3. **Wellness stipend** ($200/year) - Hard to track, no accountability, individual purchases only
4. **Wellable platform** ($48K/year) - Generic step challenges, 31% MAU (declining), poor mobile experience

**Pain Points**:
- **Critical - No ROI Visibility**: Can't prove wellness program impact on retention or productivity. CFO asking "What are we getting for $120K/year?" and Sarah has no quantitative answer. Exit interviews show "feeling isolated" as #2 reason for leaving, but can't connect wellness engagement to retention.
  - *Evidence*: "Every year it's the same conversation: 'That's a lot of money, Sarah. What are we getting?' I need better ammunition than 'People say they like it' in our surveys."

- **High - Declining Participation**: All programs start strong (80% week 1) but drop to 20% by week 8. Employees sign up but don't stay engaged. No social accountability or peer pressure in remote environment.
  - *Evidence*: Virtual happy hours went from 80% to 20% attendance. Wellable dropped from 48% MAU at launch to 31% currently.

- **High - One-Size-Fits-All Fails Everyone**: Employee fitness levels range from "marathon runner" to "can't do a push-up." Generic programs bore advanced users and intimidate beginners. Equipment constraints vary wildly (home gym vs. nothing).
  - *Evidence*: Monthly wellness newsletter has 18% open rate, 3% click-through. Employees tried early programs, got generic experience, now ignore communications.

- **Medium - Asynchronous Coordination Challenges**: 6 time zones make synchronous activities impossible. Can't do live classes or real-time team workouts. Need activities that work async but still feel social.
  - *Evidence*: "Zoom fatigue is real—people are exhausted by end of day." Virtual happy hours only worked for certain time zones.

- **Medium - Admin Time Sink**: Managing Wellable + Headspace + Calm + Gympass + wellness stipend tracking takes 20+ hours per quarter. Constantly troubleshooting access issues, sending reminders, creating content.
  - *Evidence*: "I don't have bandwidth for month-long implementations" or complex ongoing management.

### Emotional Job

**Desired Feelings**:
- **Confident in Decisions**: Wants to feel confident defending wellness program to CFO with data, not gut feelings
  - *Why this matters*: Career depends on being seen as strategic partner, not just "soft benefits" administrator

- **Proud of Culture**: Wants to feel proud that remote employees have strong connections and don't feel lonely
  - *Why this matters*: Believes in remote-first mission, doesn't want to lose culture war to "return to office" companies

- **Efficient & In Control**: Wants to feel like wellness program runs itself without constant firefighting
  - *Why this matters*: Small team, can't spend all day on wellness admin when also managing recruiting, onboarding, performance mgmt

- **Proactive vs. Reactive**: Wants to get ahead of turnover with preventive culture-building, not just react to exit interviews
  - *Why this matters*: "Feeling isolated" shows up in exit interviews after employees already left—too late

**Avoided Feelings**:
- **Defensive & Embarrassed**: Dreads quarterly budget reviews where she can't justify wellness spend with data
  - *Context*: CFO is skeptical of "soft" benefits; Sarah feels like she's always making emotional appeals vs. business case

- **Helpless**: Hates watching engagement drop and not knowing why or how to fix it
  - *Context*: "People sign up for wellness goals but abandon them"—no visibility into why or how to intervene

- **Wasteful**: Feels guilty about unused gym reimbursements and low-engagement programs
  - *Context*: Unused gym budget rolls over, 72% of employees don't use $50/month benefit—"waste"

### Social Job

**Desired Perception**:
- **Strategic Business Partner** (not just HR admin): Wants executives to see her as ROI-driven, data-informed leader
- **Culture Champion**: Wants employees to see her as genuinely caring about their wellbeing and connection
- **Innovator**: Wants to be seen as finding creative solutions to remote work challenges (not just copying what others do)

**Social Context**:
- Reports to CEO (cares about culture, retention, being "best remote company")
- Works with CFO (skeptical, data-driven, wants to cut "nice to have" spend)
- Influences 500 employees (mixture of trust and skepticism about wellness programs)
- Compares self to HR leaders at competitor companies ("What do they spend?" "What works for them?")

**Status Concerns**:
- **Career Trajectory**: Wants promotion to VP of People within 2 years—needs wins to demonstrate strategic impact
- **Professional Reputation**: Active in HR leadership communities—embarrassed when peers ask "How's your wellness program going?"
- **Internal Credibility**: If wellness program gets cut, seen as failure to demonstrate value
- **Employee Trust**: Past wellness programs failed—employees now skeptical when Sarah announces "new initiative"

## Current Workflow & Triggers

### Job Triggers (When this job arises)

**Reactive Triggers** (Responding to problems):
1. **Exit Interview Pattern**: Another employee cites "feeling disconnected" in exit interview
2. **Engagement Survey Results**: Quarterly eNPS drops, especially in remote engagement questions
3. **Budget Review Meetings**: CFO questions wellness program ROI every quarter
4. **Low Participation Alerts**: Wellable dashboard shows declining MAU

**Proactive Triggers** (Planning ahead):
5. **Annual Benefits Planning**: Q4 planning for next year's wellness strategy
6. **Vendor Contract Renewals**: Wellable contract expires Dec 31, forces decision
7. **Competitive Pressure**: Hears about cool wellness programs at other companies
8. **New Hire Onboarding**: Explaining wellness benefits to new employees

**Current Trigger Context**:
Sarah is in acute pain right now due to **Compelling Event**: Q1 2026 budget review on January 15. CFO flagged $120K wellness spend for review ("Either show ROI or we're cutting it"). Wellable contract expires December 31. Decision needed by December 1 (4 weeks away).

### Typical Workflow (Current State)

**Step 1: Research & Evaluation** (Current: 2-3 months)
- Google "best corporate wellness platforms"
- Ask HR peers in Slack communities
- Schedule vendor demos (takes 4-6 weeks to see 5 vendors)
- Create comparison spreadsheet (features, pricing, integrations)
- **Pain**: Time-consuming, hard to compare apples-to-apples, vendors all claim same results

**Step 2: Internal Selling** (Current: 1-2 months)
- Build business case PowerPoint for CEO/CFO
- Estimate participation (usually overly optimistic)
- Get budget approval (< $75K easy, > $75K requires CFO buy-in)
- **Pain**: Hard to project ROI without data from current solution

**Step 3: Implementation** (Current: 1-3 months)
- IT integration (SSO, HRIS sync, wearable connections)
- Content setup (challenges, messaging, branding)
- Training for wellness team
- **Pain**: "I don't have bandwidth for month-long implementations"

**Step 4: Launch & Promotion** (Current: 2-4 weeks)
- All-hands announcement
- Email campaigns
- Manager training ("Encourage your teams to participate")
- **Pain**: High initial excitement (80% signup) but no sustained engagement strategy

**Step 5: Ongoing Management** (Current: Continuous)
- Send weekly reminders/newsletters (18% open rate)
- Troubleshoot access issues
- Create manual challenges (time-consuming)
- Answer employee questions
- **Pain**: 20+ hours per quarter, mostly low-value admin work

**Step 6: Quarterly Reporting** (Current: 20 hours per quarter)
- Pull participation data from platform
- Send satisfaction surveys (30% response rate)
- Manually correlate with HR metrics (retention, eNPS)
- Create PowerPoint for executive team
- **Pain**: Weak data, can't prove causation, CFO skeptical

### Desired Workflow (Future State)

Sarah's ideal solution would:
1. **Fast Evaluation**: See it in action with real demo/trial in days, not months of vendor demos
2. **Quick Setup**: Implementation in < 2 weeks with minimal IT lift
3. **Self-Service Launch**: Employees can onboard themselves without Sarah's team hand-holding
4. **Automated Engagement**: System creates challenges, sends notifications, gamifies participation without manual effort
5. **Real-Time ROI Dashboard**: Always-on dashboard showing participation, engagement trends, correlation to retention
6. **Minimal Admin**: < 5 hours per quarter (vs. 20+ currently)

## Success Criteria & Buying Factors

### What "Success" Looks Like to Sarah

**Short-Term (First 90 days)**:
- **> 60% participation rate** within first month (vs. 31% with Wellable)
- **Positive employee feedback**: "This is actually fun" instead of "corporate homework"
- **Easy to use**: < 5 support tickets per week (vs. 15-20 with Wellable)
- **Works across time zones**: Employees in all 6 time zones actively participating

**Medium-Term (6 months)**:
- **Sustained engagement**: 60%+ participation maintained (not dropping to 20%)
- **Team bonding evidence**: Employees mentioning teammates in feedback ("I love competing with my team")
- **ROI dashboard live**: Can pull retention correlation report anytime
- **Admin time reduced**: < 5 hours per quarter managing program

**Long-Term (12 months)**:
- **Quantifiable retention impact**: "Employees who participate in team challenges stay 25% longer"
- **Budget secured**: CFO approves wellness spend for following year without debate
- **Expansion request**: Employees asking for more challenges, features
- **Benchmark leader**: Better participation than peer companies (Sarah can brag in HR communities)

### Buying Factors

**Must-Haves** (Deal-breakers if missing):
1. **Team Challenge Feature**: Create team-based fitness competitions (not just individual)
2. **ROI Dashboard**: Show participation rates, engagement trends, retention correlation
3. **Mobile-First**: Works great on phones (where employees actually are)
4. **Inclusive Design**: All fitness levels, works without equipment
5. **Fast Implementation**: < 2 weeks to launch (doesn't require months of IT work)
6. **Under $75K/year**: Stays within Sarah's decision authority

**Nice-to-Haves** (Differentiators):
- **AI Personalization**: Adjusts to individual fitness levels and constraints
- **Wearable Integration**: Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin (employees already own these)
- **Social Features**: Cheer for teammates, trash talk, celebration moments
- **White-Label**: Feels like "Acme Wellness" not "Generic Vendor Platform"
- **Async-First Design**: Works across time zones without requiring synchronous participation

**Deal-Breakers** (Automatic disqualification):
- **Requires equipment purchases**: Can't ask employees to buy $1,500 bikes or $500 equipment
- **Per-user pricing for all employees**: Won't pay for 500 employees if only 300 use it
- **Desktop-only experience**: "Nobody will use it"
- **Complex implementation**: "I don't have bandwidth for month-long implementations"
- **No ROI metrics**: Can't justify to CFO without data
