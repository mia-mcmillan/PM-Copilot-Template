# Acme Corp - HR Director - JTBD Interview

## Participant Profile
- **Name**: Sarah Martinez (fictional)
- **Role**: Director of People Operations
- **Company**: Acme Corp (500 employees, B2B SaaS, HR software)
- **Location**: Remote-first company, headquarters in Austin, TX
- **Background**: 8 years in HR, 3 years at Acme
- **Challenge**: Maintaining wellness and culture in distributed workforce

## Interview Context
**Date**: November 15, 2025
**Duration**: 45 minutes
**Format**: Video call (Zoom)
**Interviewer**: Product Team

## Executive Summary
Sarah is facing budget scrutiny for the company's wellness program ($120K/year) while struggling with poor engagement. Remote employees feel disconnected, and traditional wellness solutions (gym reimbursements, virtual happy hours) aren't working. She needs to demonstrate ROI to secure Q1 2026 budget renewal while finding activities that actually engage distributed teams.

---

## Key Jobs-to-be-Done

### Job 1: Justify Wellness ROI to Executive Team

**When I** need to renew our wellness program budget in Q1 2026,
**I want to** show measurable impact on retention, productivity, and healthcare costs,
**So I can** secure continued funding without having to defend every dollar.

**Current Solution**: Manual tracking via quarterly surveys + spreadsheet analysis
- Survey response rate: ~30% (declining)
- Manually correlate survey data with HR metrics
- Create PowerPoint presentations for executive team
- Time investment: 20+ hours per quarter

**Pain Points**:
- **No real-time visibility**: Can only report on what happened last quarter
- **Weak correlation**: Hard to prove wellness → retention causation
- **CFO skepticism**: "We're spending $240 per employee per year—what's the return?"
- **Comparison shopping**: CFO keeps asking "What do competitors spend?"
- **Data gaps**: Can't track who actually uses the program vs. who just enrolled

**Desired Outcome**:
- Automated dashboard I can pull up anytime showing:
  - Participation rates by department
  - Correlation to retention (even if directional)
  - Benchmark data from similar companies
  - Healthcare cost trends (if available)
- Ability to say: "73% of employees who engaged with wellness stayed vs. 58% who didn't"

**Quote**:
> "Every year it's the same conversation: 'That's a lot of money, Sarah. What are we getting?' I need better ammunition than 'People say they like it' in our surveys."

---

### Job 2: Boost Remote Team Engagement & Culture

**When I** see remote employees feeling disconnected and lonely,
**I want to** create opportunities for team bonding that don't require physical presence,
**So I can** maintain company culture and reduce turnover in our distributed team.

**Current Solutions Tried**:

1. **Virtual Happy Hours** (abandoned)
   - Week 1: 80% attendance → Week 8: 20% attendance
   - "Zoom fatigue is real—people are exhausted by end of day"
   - Introverts hate them, extroverts tolerate them

2. **Gym Membership Reimbursement** ($50/month)
   - 28% of employees use it
   - Post-COVID, many don't want to go to gyms
   - Doesn't help team bonding (everyone goes alone)
   - Unused budget rolls over (waste)

3. **Wellness Stipend** ($200/year for "wellness activities")
   - Hard to track, people buy weird stuff
   - One person bought a massage chair for their dog
   - No team interaction, just individual purchases

**Pain Points**:
- **Asynchronous work = disconnection**: Team members in 6 time zones
- **No organic watercooler moments**: Remote kills casual relationship building
- **Visible loneliness**: Exit interviews cite "feeling isolated" as #2 reason for leaving
- **Competing with return-to-office**: Other companies luring talent with "come back to the office for culture"
- **Lack of accountability**: People sign up for wellness goals but abandon them (no peer pressure)

**Desired Outcome**:
- Activity that:
  - Works across time zones (asynchronous OK)
  - Creates natural competition/cooperation (gamification)
  - Builds relationships without forced Zoom calls
  - Measurable (I can see who's engaging)
  - Inclusive (not just for gym rats)
- Evidence I can point to: "Our team challenges had 75% participation vs. 20% for happy hours"

**Quote**:
> "We're a remote-first company, but that shouldn't mean people feel alone. I need something that brings teams together around wellness without another damn Zoom meeting."

---

### Job 3: Personalize Wellness Without Hiring More People

**When I** see one-size-fits-all wellness programs failing diverse employee needs,
**I want to** offer personalized options without expanding my 2-person wellness team,
**So I can** improve engagement without ballooning headcount/budget.

**Current Reality**:
- **Demographics**: Ages 24-62, fitness levels from "marathon runner" to "can't do a push-up"
- **Constraints vary**: Some have home gyms, some have nothing, some only have 10 minutes/day
- **Interests differ**: Yoga lovers, weightlifters, runners, haters of all exercise
- **Generic program fails everyone**:
  - Advanced people are bored
  - Beginners are intimidated
  - People with no equipment can't participate

**Current "Solution"**: Send monthly wellness newsletter with generic tips
- Open rate: 18%
- Click-through rate: 3%
- Impact: Unknown (probably zero)

**Pain Points**:
- **Can't scale personal coaching**: Would need 1 wellness coach per 50 employees (unaffordable)
- **Generic = irrelevant**: "Do 30 minutes of cardio" doesn't help someone who doesn't know what that means
- **Lost trust**: Employees tried early programs, got generic experience, now ignore us
- **Admin burden**: Managing different vendors (Gympass, Headspace, Calm, etc.) is time-consuming

**Desired Outcome**:
- **AI-powered personalization** that:
  - Asks about constraints (time, equipment, fitness level)
  - Adjusts difficulty based on performance
  - Offers alternatives when someone can't do something
  - Surfaces relevant content without me curating it
- **Feels personal** without requiring human coaches
- **Self-serve** so employees don't need my team's help

**Quote**:
> "I can't afford a personal trainer for every employee, but I also can't keep sending 'here's 10 wellness tips' emails that nobody reads. There has to be a middle path."

## Competitive Context & Alternatives Considered

### Tools Evaluated:

**1. Gympass (2023, abandoned after 6 months)**
- **Why tried**: Seemed like easy button—access to gyms nationwide
- **Why failed**:
  - $20/user/month for all employees (including non-users) = $120K/year waste
  - Only 22% ever activated accounts
  - Doesn't work for remote employees without gyms nearby
  - Zero team bonding component
- **Lesson**: "Access to gyms" ≠ solving engagement problem

**2. Wellable (currently using, considering cancellation)**
- **Why using**: Good admin dashboard, integrates with wearables, challenges feature
- **What works**:
  - Dashboard shows participation by department
  - Step challenges somewhat popular
- **What doesn't work**:
  - Content is generic and boring ("Walk 10,000 steps!")
  - Challenges feel forced ("Submit a selfie doing yoga")
  - No AI/personalization—everyone gets same experience
  - Mobile app is clunky (1.8 stars on App Store)
  - Employees complain it's "corporate homework"
- **Price**: $8/user/month = $48K/year
- **Usage**: 31% monthly active users (down from 48% at launch)
- **Renewal decision**: Up for renewal in Q1 2026, on the fence

**3. Peloton for Business (evaluated, didn't buy)**
- **Why considered**: Love the content, highly engaging
- **Why rejected**:
  - Requires $1,495 bike or $2,495 treadmill per user
  - Only 8% of employees said they'd buy equipment (survey)
  - Would cost $600K+ to outfit interested employees
  - Creates haves/have-nots divide (not everyone can afford/fit equipment)
- **Wish**: "If Peloton made a no-equipment version, I'd buy it tomorrow"

**4. Headspace/Calm (using, limited engagement)**
- **Why using**: Covers mental wellness, board likes it
- **Usage**: 19% of employees have opened the app
- **Issue**: Passive consumption, no team element, hard to measure impact

## What Would Make Sarah a Champion?

**Must-Haves:**
1. **ROI Dashboard**: Show me participation, engagement trends, and correlation to retention
2. **Team Challenges**: Create team-based fitness competitions that work across time zones
3. **Inclusive Design**: Work for all fitness levels, with or without equipment
4. **Mobile-First**: Employees live on their phones, not desktops
5. **No Admin Burden**: I don't have time to set up challenges, send reminders, or troubleshoot

**Nice-to-Haves:**
1. **AI Personalization**: Adjusts to individual constraints and goals
2. **Wearable Integration**: Syncs with Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin (employees already have these)
3. **Social Features**: Let people cheer each other on without forced Zoom calls
4. **White-Label**: Make it feel like Acme's program, not "yet another vendor"

**Deal-Breakers:**
1. **Requires equipment purchases**: Can't ask employees to buy $1,500 bikes
2. **Per-user pricing for non-users**: Won't pay for people who don't use it
3. **Desktop-only**: Nobody will use it
4. **Complex setup**: I don't have bandwidth for month-long implementations
